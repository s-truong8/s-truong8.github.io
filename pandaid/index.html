Where this comes from

The advice in the game follows Canadian first-aid guidance (checked in November 2026):

  • Phone 9-1-1 and get an AED (or shout for one). Put the phone on speaker if you are alone. [1] [2]
  • Push hard and fast in the centre of the chest, with two hands for an adult. [1] [2]
  • 100 to 120 compressions per minute: the beat you follow on the compressions screen. [1] [2]
  • At least 5 cm deep for an adult, letting the chest come back up between pushes. [2]
  • Hands-only CPR (no breaths) is acceptable for an adult; dispatchers now coach adults this way. [2] [3]
  • Don't stop until help takes over, an AED is found, or the person responds. [1] [2]

Briefing

How to play

  1. 01

    Camera check

    Kneel next to a cushion, webcam at your side. We check it can see your arms.

  2. 02

    The right calls

    Is it safe? Does he respond? Is he breathing? Who do you call? Answer before the clock runs out.

  3. 03

    Chest compressions

    Push on the cushion. The webcam reads your rhythm, depth and arms, and coaches you live.

  4. 04

    Your grade

    The ambulance takes over. You get a score and one tip for next time.

You need: a firm cushion, a webcam that sees you from the side, space to kneel, sound on, and two minutes.
